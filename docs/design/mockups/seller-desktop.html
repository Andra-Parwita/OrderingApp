Seller desktop layouts

Draft mock-up for the owner to choose between A1 and A2 (D-030). Static, light mode (one dark preview at the end). Sample data is the same in every frame. Frames are 1280 x 800 and scale to fit the page.

Design rules (for low tech literacy), applied in every frame
  • Plain words, no jargon (“Needs confirming”, not “Pending”).
  • Verbs on buttons (“Confirm order”, “Send link on WhatsApp”).
  • One obvious primary action per view.
  • Icons always come with a text label.
  • No everyday action hidden in a “…” menu.
  • Body text 16 px or larger; targets 44 px or larger.
  • Actions always in the same place (A1: bottom of the panel; A2: top of the pane).
  • Generous but not wasteful spacing; a table has at most 6 columns.
  • Status is always text (colour is only a tint); current menu item is bold with a bar.
  • Contrast: palette text, textMuted and outline tokens, AA or better.

Comparison

A1 · Table + slide-over

  • Strengths: real desktop look; the whole week scans like a sheet; one thing on screen at a time; the six columns are labelled in plain words.
  • Risks for low-tech users: must open and close the panel for every order; panel hides the list, so “what was the next one?” needs a close first; the table row has to be seen as clickable.
  • Best for: checking and finding orders; sellers who handle orders one at a time and like a quiet screen.

A2 · Three columns

  • Strengths: no open/close; click the next order and act at once; the actions never move; close to the split view the seller has now.
  • Risks for low-tech users: three regions at once is busier; list is narrower, so chips wrap into four rows and the list shows fewer orders (about 5); the detail pane is wide with much empty space.
  • Best for: working through all the “Needs confirming” orders in a row on a large screen.
Recommendation: A1. It is calmer and looks like a desktop app, which was the owner's complaint, and it shows more orders at once (8 against about 5). Its one cost is the open and close step; that can be softened with a labelled “Next order” button in the panel if wanted. Choose A2 if the seller mostly confirms many orders in a row.
